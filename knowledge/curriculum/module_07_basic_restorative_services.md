# Module 07: Basic Restorative Services

## Learning objectives

- Apply restorative principles that preserve resident function.
- Perform safe ambulation, ROM, and positioning support.
- Document functional response and report decline quickly.

## Regulatory references (Texas and federal)

- **42 CFR §483.25** - Quality of care and highest practicable function.
- **42 CFR §483.24(c)** - Activities of daily living assistance and maintenance.
- **26 TAC §556.2** - Nurse aide restorative-care competency expectations.

## Chapter 1: Restorative principles and CNA role

### Core instruction points
- Encourage resident participation rather than replacement care.
- Reinforce interdisciplinary restorative goals.
- Track functional baseline and trends.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Differentiates restorative support from total-care approach.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Uses cueing that promotes resident participation in ADLs.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** States when decline triggers immediate nurse report.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. Restorative care goal is: **Answer: maximize function and independence.**
2. CNA should do task for resident whenever possible? **Answer: no, encourage participation when safe.**
3. Functional decline should be: **Answer: documented and reported promptly.**

**Case scenario**
- Resident with recent weakness is over-assisted during grooming.

**Remediation notes**
- Practice graded-assistance prompts.
- Reevaluate baseline documentation habits.

## Chapter 2: Mobility support and safe ambulation

### Core instruction points
- Prepare environment and assistive devices before movement.
- Use gait-belt and body mechanics correctly.
- Stop and escalate if dizziness, pain, or instability occurs.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Completes pre-ambulation safety setup with zero critical misses.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Demonstrates safe gait-belt ambulation technique.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Initiates stop-and-escalate protocol for instability signs.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. Before ambulation, CNA must verify: **Answer: care plan, assist level, and equipment safety.**
2. Resident knees buckle during walk. CNA should: **Answer: assist safely to seated/secure position and notify nurse.**
3. Purpose of gait belt is: **Answer: controlled support and fall-risk reduction.**

**Case scenario**
- Resident with walker reports sudden fatigue halfway to dining room.

**Remediation notes**
- Repeat ambulation path simulation with safety checkpoints.
- Reassess transfer-to-ambulation transitions.

## Chapter 3: ROM and positioning routines

### Core instruction points
- Distinguish active and passive ROM tasks per care plan.
- Move joints slowly through safe range without forcing.
- Reposition regularly to protect skin and comfort.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Demonstrates ordered ROM sequence with proper alignment.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Stops ROM immediately for pain/spasm and reports.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Repositions resident using pressure-injury prevention principles.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. ROM should be performed how? **Answer: slow, controlled, and pain-aware.**
2. If resident reports sharp pain during ROM: **Answer: stop and notify nurse.**
3. Repositioning frequency supports prevention of: **Answer: pressure injury and contracture progression.**

**Case scenario**
- Learner completes passive ROM and must adjust when pain is reported.

**Remediation notes**
- Retrain on joint-protection mechanics.
- Repeat ROM documentation with tolerance detail.

## Chapter 4: Restorative documentation and reporting

### Core instruction points
- Record distance, repetitions, assistance level, and tolerance.
- Report abrupt decline, refusal, or new pain.
- Use objective trend language for team follow-up.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Completes restorative note with measurable performance data.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Escalates functional decline with same-shift nurse notification.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Distinguishes refusal from inability in documentation.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. Best restorative charting includes: **Answer: measurable outcomes and resident response.**
2. New weakness during transfer should be: **Answer: reported immediately.**
3. "Resident lazy today" is: **Answer: unacceptable subjective charting.**

**Case scenario**
- Week-over-week ambulation distance drops by 40%; learner reports trend.

**Remediation notes**
- Practice trend-focused charting examples.
- Repeat report handoff emphasizing decline indicators.
