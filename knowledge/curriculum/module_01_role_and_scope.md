# Module 01: Role of the Nurse Aide

## Learning objectives

- Define CNA scope of practice in Texas long-term care settings.
- Apply delegation and chain-of-command expectations for safe care.
- Document and report resident observations using objective language.

## Regulatory references (Texas and federal)

- **26 TAC §556.2 and §556.12** - Nurse aide training and competency expectations.
- **Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 250** - Long-term care resident safety and reporting responsibilities.
- **42 CFR §483.35 and §483.95** - Sufficient nursing services and staff training requirements.

## Chapter 1: Scope and accountability

### Core instruction points
- Distinguish CNA tasks from nurse-only tasks before accepting an assignment.
- Verify competency, resident stability, and available supervision.
- Recognize legal responsibility for omissions, delays, and inaccurate reporting.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** States 3 in-scope CNA tasks and 3 out-of-scope tasks without prompting.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Uses escalation language when asked to perform a nurse-only intervention.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Identifies who to notify first for a sudden resident status change.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. A CNA is asked to give a PRN medication. What is the safest response? **Answer: decline and notify the nurse.**
2. Which action is within CNA scope? **Answer: collect and report objective vital signs.**
3. Scope decisions should be based on what first? **Answer: care plan, training, and resident safety.**

**Case scenario**
- During evening rounds, a resident reports chest pressure. Learner identifies immediate reporting chain and safety actions.

**Remediation notes**
- Re-teach scope boundaries using facility task matrix.
- Repeat simulation with escalating urgency cues.

## Chapter 2: Delegation and chain of command

### Core instruction points
- Confirm assignment details: resident, task, timing, and expected outcome.
- Clarify unclear instructions before beginning care.
- Escalate urgent findings immediately and document who was notified.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Uses closed-loop communication to repeat back delegated instructions.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Demonstrates escalation from CNA to charge nurse in a time-critical scenario.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Documents time, action, and recipient of report accurately.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. The nurse gives a vague assignment. What should the CNA do first? **Answer: request clarification.**
2. First report for acute shortness of breath goes to whom? **Answer: supervising/charge nurse immediately.**
3. Delegation accountability remains with whom? **Answer: licensed nurse.**

**Case scenario**
- Learner receives unclear transfer instructions and must clarify before resident movement.

**Remediation notes**
- Practice SBAR-style reporting script.
- Repeat delegation drills until communication is complete and concise.

## Chapter 3: Professional boundaries and ethics

### Core instruction points
- Maintain therapeutic boundaries with residents and families.
- Protect privacy in hallways, elevators, and digital communication.
- Resolve dignity-versus-safety conflicts by escalating concerns, not improvising unsafe care.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Identifies boundary violations in 3 role-play examples.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Demonstrates HIPAA-safe communication in public-area scenarios.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Selects escalation pathway for ethical conflict without delaying care.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. Discussing resident details on social media is: **Answer: prohibited.**
2. A resident refuses care. CNA should: **Answer: respect refusal, ensure safety, notify nurse.**
3. Ethical care begins with: **Answer: dignity and informed choice.**

**Case scenario**
- Family asks for confidential details in hallway; learner responds using privacy-protective script.

**Remediation notes**
- Review examples of therapeutic vs non-therapeutic language.
- Coach learner on consent and refusal documentation workflow.

## Chapter 4: Documentation and handoff fundamentals

### Core instruction points
- Chart objective findings only (what was seen, heard, measured, done).
- Include timestamp, action, and nurse notification details.
- Use concise handoff updates to support continuity of care.

### Skill checklist (pass/fail)
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Completes a charting sample with objective language and no assumptions.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Provides a 60-second handoff including safety risks and pending tasks.
- [ ] **PASS/FAIL:** Corrects a flawed chart note containing judgmental language.

### Assessment content
**Quiz bank**
1. Best documentation phrase: **Answer: "Resident ambulated 20 ft with gait belt; reported dizziness; nurse notified at 1010."**
2. Charting should be completed when? **Answer: promptly after care.**
3. Subjective interpretation in charting is: **Answer: unacceptable.**

**Case scenario**
- End-of-shift handoff includes fall risk, toileting status, and recent symptom report.

**Remediation notes**
- Rebuild chart note with objective verbs and measurable detail.
- Practice handoff until key safety points are reliably included.
